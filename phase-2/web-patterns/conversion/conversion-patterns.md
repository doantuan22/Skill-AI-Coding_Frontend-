# Conversion patterns

Conversion is earned by clarity and proof, not by repetition or pressure. Actions and flows are locked in Phase 1; these patterns govern presentation. Button hierarchy follows the [button grammar](../../visual-language/components/buttons.md).

| Pattern | When | Rules | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary action at decision points** | Every page with a conversion goal | One primary label used consistently; repeat after major proof sections, not after every section | Identical CTA banner after every block |
| **Inline CTA** | Content/editorial pages | Text or secondary button after value is delivered | Interrupting reading with modal prompts |
| **Sticky action bar** | Long product/booking pages on mobile | Shows price/summary + action; does not cover content or consent UI | Two sticky bars; covering form fields |
| **Pricing table** | Multiple tiers | Real features, clear billing period, recommended tier justified; compare in a table on desktop, cards on mobile | Fake discounts; hidden fees; decoy tiers without disclosure |
| **Sign-up / waitlist** | Minimal products, launches | Minimal fields (as locked), clear privacy note, success state | Asking for data beyond the locked form |
| **Reassurance near action** | Transactions (booking, purchase, trial) | Price, cancellation/refund, security, support adjacent to the action | Reassurance buried in footer |
| **Closing CTA** | End of narrative pages | Summarize the value in one line + action | Gradient card by default; generic "Ready to get started?" without specificity |

Urgency, scarcity or social pressure must be truthful and sourced; fabricated urgency is a dark pattern and is rejected.
